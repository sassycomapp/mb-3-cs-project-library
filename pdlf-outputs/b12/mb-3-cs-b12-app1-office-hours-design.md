## Problem Statement

A consulting or service business that wants to operate professionally online must buy and wire together five to seven separate tools — website and hosting, booking, payment gateway, CRM, email marketing, invoicing — each with its own subscription, learning curve and support. The tools do not talk to each other: the owner re-types data between them, holds client records in three or four systems, and still has no reliable view of the business. On marketplaces the business's customers are effectively the platform's customers, with commissions to 23.5%. Five compounding parts: money, integration, technical burden, data, ownership (concept §2).

## Demand Evidence

- The founder lived the problem: years attempting to establish an online business, "not as an observer, but as the person sitting in front of the screen" (concept §3). After speaking with other small business owners, "the pattern was consistent" (concept §3).
- The status quo's measurable cost: $324/month at list prices for a team of six across Squarespace, Acuity, HubSpot, FreshBooks and Jasper, before setup, with per-seat fees on every further staff member (concept §6.2).
- Why now: digital service businesses are normal; AI is expected; marketplace fatigue is growing; Stripe does not accept South African businesses directly (concept §4.6).
- Not in the record (ruled by David this session): no named individual, no interviews, no payments, no usage figures. The strongest behavioural claim the documents make is "the pattern was consistent" (concept §3). The designed demand test is the wedge: ten real founding clients (concept §10.2, BRD §8 KPI) and demo apps that show the whole product before anyone pays (F-36, ADR no-free-trial).

## Status Quo

A team of six buys Squarespace Core $23, Acuity Standard $34, HubSpot Starter (6 seats) $120, FreshBooks Lite + 5 members $78, Jasper Pro $69 — $324/month (concept §6.2), excluding team inbox, voice, backups, legal pages, reviews, portal and accounts. The human is the integration: a booking creates no contact, a payment creates no invoice, and client data lives in three or four systems (concept §2). The marketplace alternative leases the customer relationship and lists competitors on the business's page (concept §5, §6.3).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The person: the owner-operator of a small consulting or service business — solo or small team, South Africa first, global from launch (concept §4.1–4.4). Title: owner. What gets them "promoted": more booked clients and getting paid on time (concept §4.5, needs 1–2). What gets them "fired": losing clients and money to fragmented tools that never form a complete system (concept §2, §3). What keeps them up at night: juggling and paying for separate booking, invoicing, website, email and CRM services that do not work together (concept §2, §4.5).

The wedge is not a reduced feature set: it is the first ten real businesses from the founder's direct network and warm referrals, onboarded on the full product at the standard price, willing to say so publicly (concept §10.2). Capacity is a hard 100 clients (concept §1, BRD AC-12).

## Constraints

- One app and one database per client; never multi-tenant (ADR dependency-based-not-multi-tenant).
- The server holds authority over identity, ownership and every rule (spec-anvil-platform-standards §10).
- All client code and theme/assets are public (context pack, item 4); secrets only in the Vault (spec-vault-system).
- Every client instance on its own Mybizz-owned Anvil Business account; the Hobby plan never used (architecture reference §4).
- The in-app notification surface is specified by spec-mastertemplate-notification-system §8: bell icon and badge, a notification feed with a Load-more cursor, toasts suppressed for marketing priority, a preferences centre, and 30–60-second polling.
- POPIA everywhere; GDPR when EU customers are served; PCI DSS when payments are taken — SAQ A, card data never touches the instance (spec-regulatory-compliance-baseline).
- Platform: Anvil.works, Material 3, one release through master_template reaches every client (architecture reference §§1, 13–14).
- No scope changes from this session: the 36 features, pricing and architecture are fixed by the documents (PRD §7: nothing deferred).

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Bookings-core first (minimal viable)
Launch with F-01, F-10, F-11, F-15 only; add the rest after revenue. Effort S; Risk Low. Fastest to a first paying client. Rejected by the documents: PRD §7 defers nothing; F-36 and BR-26 require the whole product in the demos; ADR tiers-model forbids feature tiers under the one price.

### Approach B: the five-app full-platform build on the scaffold as it stands (ideal architecture)
Complete the recorded migration (P1–P13), add the packages and forms the scaffold specifies, replace the Brevo integration module with SES, consolidate the duplicated Vault modules. Effort L; Risk Medium. Matches every document; the demos show the whole product; one release reaches every client.

### Approach C: marketplace entry (creative/lateral)
Harvest demand Booksy-style, then convert clients to isolated instances. Effort M; Risk High. Rejected by the documents: concept §5 — "Mybizz CS is not a marketplace"; the ownership architecture is the differentiator; BRD AC-1 fixes the five-app model.

### (From the PDLF draft, recorded for completeness)
Restructure-first (split common/trade packages now) — rejected: the scaffold's rule that existing packages are never renamed during active development; features-only-defer-hygiene — rejected: leaves an ADR-violating module in the code and defers a marking Step 12 must make.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B — because the documents fix the scope and the architecture (PRD §7; BRD AC-1; concept §5), and the recorded migration plus the scaffold define a completion path that reuses everything already built.
