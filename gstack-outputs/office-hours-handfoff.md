# Design: Mybizz - Integrated Business Operating System

## Problem Statement

Small service business owners (physiotherapists, personal trainers, consultants) are losing money because they cannot tie together the pieces needed to run an online business. They use disjointed tools: WhatsApp for booking requests, Google Sheets for schedules, separate sheets for payments, bank apps to verify transactions. One booking takes 11 minutes of manual admin. They're turning away clients due to double-booking. At 40% efficiency, they're losing business daily and facing bankruptcy within 4-5 months.

## Demand Evidence

- **Cash in hand:** Multiple clients have paid upfront to build the system
- **Specific client:** Buks Van-Blerk, owner-operator of a physiotherapy practice, has been "pestering almost daily"
- **Real consequence:** Bankruptcy in 4-5 months if not solved
- **Observed workflow:** Watched Buks spend 11 minutes on one booking, manually copying data between WhatsApp, Google Sheets, and bank app
- **Revenue impact:** Turning away clients due to double-booking

## Status Quo

Buks runs a physiotherapy practice. End-of-day admin session:
- WhatsApp open for booking requests
- Google Sheet for schedule
- Separate sheet tracking payments
- Bank app open to verify EFT payments
- New booking comes in: he copies client name to schedule, manually checks for slot, replies on WhatsApp, notes follow-up on payment
- Whole thing takes 11 minutes for one booking
- He built a functioning system out of pure habit and genuinely doesn't believe anything better is realistic for his size

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target User:** Small service business owners (physiotherapists, personal trainers, consultants) who:
- Have some technical capability but lack "management savvy"
- Are currently using 5-7 disjointed tools
- Are losing money daily due to inefficiency
- Are facing bankruptcy or severe revenue loss within months

**Narrowest Wedge:** Website → Book → Pay → Confirm → Remind
- Client finds business online
- Books a service
- Pays in the same flow
- Receives confirmation
- Gets automatic reminder
- Owner sees booking in calendar and payment in account

This is the bleeding stopper. Buks can have website, booking, and payment live and taking real money before anything else is built.

**Note:** This is not a new idea being validated. It is a fully scoped, designed, and documented system that has been months in preparation. The wedge question has been answered: the booking and payment flow is the core, and the full platform is already built around it. The wireframes exist. The schema exists. The architecture is decided. The ADRs are written.

## Constraints

- **Must work from day one:** Nothing to build, no integrations to maintain, no technical knowledge required
- **Must integrate everything:** Website, booking, payments, invoicing, client management, email marketing, public website
- **Must compete with aggregators:** Booksy, Vagaro, Mindbody are moving downmarket and will try to own the client relationship
- **Must be revenue-positive immediately:** Buks is losing money daily; the system must start taking real money this week

## Premises

1. **The bleeding stopper (Website → Book → Pay → Confirm → Remind) is the right wedge.** It stops the immediate loss of customers while building toward a full platform.

2. **Small service businesses like Buks will face increasing pressure from aggregator platforms** that own client relationships and skim revenue. Owning your own stack becomes more valuable, not less, over time.

3. **Buks (and similar founders) genuinely don't believe better is realistic for their size.** The product must prove itself through immediate revenue impact, not features.

4. **The aggregator threat is real and accelerating.** Businesses that don't build their own system now will have no leverage in 3 years.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Buy Nothing Wedge
**Summary:** Build only the bleeding stopper (Website → Book → Pay → Confirm → Remind) and nothing else. Ship V1 in 3-5 days.

**Effort:** S
**Risk:** Low
**Pros:**
- Buks can be live and taking real money this week
- Proves the wedge works before investing in the full platform
- Minimal scope = fastest time to revenue = strongest founder signal

**Cons:**
- Doesn't solve the "tie all pieces together" vision yet
- May create technical debt if V2 requires rewriting
- Risk of being perceived as "just another booking tool"

**Reuses:** Existing booking/payment APIs (Stripe, Calendly, etc.)

### Approach B: Platform from Day One
**Summary:** Build the full integrated system — website, booking, payments, CRM, invoicing, email marketing, calendar — all connected from the start.

**Effort:** XL
**Risk:** High
**Pros:**
- Delivers the complete vision Buks asked for
- No technical debt from incremental evolution
- True differentiation from aggregator platforms

**Cons:**
- Takes months to ship, not days
- Buks loses customers during development
- Scope creep risk — "just one more feature"
- Risk of over-engineering before validating the wedge

**Reuses:** Existing SaaS integrations (Zapier, Make, etc.)

### Approach C: Aggregator Wrapper
**Summary:** Build a thin layer on top of existing tools (Calendly for booking, Stripe for payments, Mailchimp for email) that provides the integrated dashboard Buks needs.

**Effort:** M
**Risk:** Medium
**Pros:**
- Fast to ship (1-2 weeks)
- Buks gets the integrated view he needs
- Leverages battle-tested tools for core functions
- Can evolve into custom features as revenue grows

**Cons:**
- Still dependent on third-party tools
- Monthly costs add up (Calendly + Stripe + Mailchimp + etc.)
- Less defensible if aggregators improve their own dashboards
- Risk of API changes breaking the glue

**Reuses:** Calendly, Stripe, Mailchimp, Google Calendar APIs

## Recommended Approach

**Rationale:** This is not a new idea being validated — it is a fully scoped, designed, and documented system that has been months in preparation. The wedge question has been answered: the booking and payment flow is the core, and the full platform is already built around it. The wireframes exist. The schema exists. The architecture is decided. The ADRs are written.

Buks needs the complete system, not a wedge. He's already lost 4-5 months of revenue to inefficiency. The aggregator threat is real and accelerating. Building incrementally creates technical debt and delays the point where he has leverage.

The full platform is the right bet because:
1. **The risk has already been taken** — Buks has paid upfront, proving demand

## Dependencies

**Business:**

- Domain registration (immediate)
